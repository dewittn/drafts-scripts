WAKING FROM INNOCENT DREAMS is [[genre]] with elements of magical realism. A cross between HADES, ARGENTINA, and THE VOLCANO DAUGHTERS, it will also appeal to readers who liked A BEAST SLINKS TOWARDS BEIJING. This coming-of-age story, complete at 110,000 words, spans twenty years and follows a boy who survived and a ghost who remembers.

Growing up, Nelson’s family loved him but looked nothing like him. He longs to meet the one person he believes will make him whole, while trying to ignore the ghost that won't leave him alone. Then a positive DNA test reveals him to be Roberto, the son of Salvadoran revolutionaries who, as an infant, was *desaparecido* during a violent government raid. At a miraculous reunion, Nelson meets a family ready to welcome him home, except for the one person who was disappeared alongside him: his mother.

After traveling to Central America, his past and present blend, thunderstorms drown out discontent, and living rooms expand to fit the entire neighborhood. Over the years, as he discovers a life taken from him, he struggles to balance the demands of his two lives, leaving him feeling *ni de aquí ni de allá*. Just as Nelson comes to terms with the cost of his disappearance, a betrayal forces him to make an impossible choice: protect the family he has dreamed of since childhood, or accept the truth his ghost and siblings have been pushing him toward.

Since the 1950s, governments throughout Central and South America have abducted more than 100,000 people as a strategy of intimidation and control. Only a small handful, almost all children, reunite with their families. Of those, a tiny fraction get a second chance to grow up with the family they were taken from.

As one of El Salvador's Disappeared Children, I am able to write about this phenomenon from personal experience: not only do I understand the ambiguous grief that comes from having someone taken, but I have also had to wrestle with the identity confusion, family separation, and cultural bifurcation that affect many Latinx, Central American, and other marginalized communities. While based on my life, this novel is cinematic and plot-driven, and it may be the first on the subject written by someone who lived through it.

I am also completing a documentary film, IDENTIFYING NELSON/BUSCANDO A ROBERTO, which revolves around a historic 2011 commemoration of El Salvador's Day of the Disappeared and offers natural co-marketing opportunities for the book.
